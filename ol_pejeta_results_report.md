# Ol Pejeta Conservancy Tree Monitoring Report

## Executive Summary

This analysis combines the tagged-tree monitoring dataset and the seedling density dataset for 2023 to 2025, using the methodology described in the accompanying brief.

Two important rules were applied during analysis:

- Drought was treated as a damage category under damager.
- Missing trees were excluded from mortality calculations because missing is not the same as dead.

Overall, the tagged-tree population showed relatively low mortality once missing records were excluded, and mean height change between 2023 and 2025 was essentially flat. The excluded plots performed much better than the non-excluded plots in both growth and survival. In the density data, mean density declined from 2023 to 2025, while height and length both increased and remained very strongly associated. Crematogaster ants were present in a minority of records, but ant presence was statistically associated with damage status.

## Tagged Trees

The tagged-tree sheet contained 1,949 trees with a recorded 2023 height. By 2025, 1,719 were still alive, 167 were dead, 49 were missing, and 14 remained unclear from the available coding.

When missing trees were excluded from mortality calculations, mortality was 8.79 percent. This gives a more defensible survival estimate because missing trees were not counted as dead.

Among trees that were alive in both years, the average height change from 2023 to 2025 was 0.09 cm, which is effectively no net change. The paired test for height change was not significant, so there is no strong evidence of an overall shift in height across the monitored tagged population during this interval.

## Height-Class Pattern

The largest share of the tagged population in 2023 fell in the 1 to 2 m class, followed by the 2 to 3 m class. By 2025, the 1 to 2 m class had declined, while the 2 to 3 m and smaller classes showed only modest movement. This suggests that the distribution remained broadly similar, but some trees moved out of the middle height class over time.

## Excluded Versus Non-Excluded Zones

The contrast between excluded and non-excluded zones was clear.

- Excluded plots had much stronger average growth, with a mean increase of 14.28 cm.
- Non-excluded plots had a slightly negative mean change of -0.77 cm.
- Mortality in excluded plots was 1.01 percent.
- Mortality in non-excluded plots was 9.22 percent.

This indicates that tree performance was substantially better in excluded plots than in the broader conservation area where large herbivores were free to move.

## Damagers and Damage Height

The dominant damagers in the tagged-tree data were elephants and rhinos, followed by other browsers and giraffes. Drought or natural drying was also recorded, but at a much lower frequency than the main wildlife damagers.

Damage height patterns were biologically consistent:

- Rhino damage was concentrated lower on the stem.
- Giraffe damage occurred at much higher heights.
- Elephant damage was spread across a wide height range.

The relationship between damager and affected height class was very strong, indicating that damage type is closely linked to the height level at which injury occurs.

## Density Data

The density dataset showed a decline in average density between 2023 and 2025.

- 2023 mean density: 2,573.2 individuals per hectare
- 2025 mean density: 2,292.2 individuals per hectare
- Mean change: -280.9 individuals per hectare

The decline was statistically significant in the matched-plot comparison, so this is not just random variation.

At the same time, average height and length increased between years:

- Mean height rose from 39.73 cm to 53.39 cm
- Mean length rose from 49.67 cm to 67.21 cm

Height and length were very strongly related, with a correlation of 0.972. This means taller seedlings and trees also tended to be longer, which is exactly the kind of tight relationship expected in these measurements.

## Ant Interaction

Crematogaster ants, defined here as BBR, RRB, TP, and AB, were present in a minority of records.

- 2023 occupancy: 13.60 percent
- 2025 occupancy: 16.21 percent

Ant presence was significantly associated with damage status in the density data. That does not by itself prove causality, but it does show that ant occupancy and damage are not independent in this dataset.

## Rainfall Context

The rainfall series provided for Ol Pejeta shows the following annual totals:

- 2023: 839.4 mm
- 2024: 1,120.8 mm
- 2025: 1,040.4 mm

Rainfall was highest in 2024 and remained relatively high in 2025 compared with 2023. This context is useful when interpreting the reduced drought-type damage and the modest overall growth response, although the available data do not support a full causal rainfall model for individual trees.

## Key Interpretation

The most important patterns are:

1. Mortality was moderate once missing trees were excluded.
2. Excluded plots performed better than non-excluded plots.
3. Elephant and rhino pressure remained the major damage pathway.
4. Drought/natural drying was present but not the dominant driver.
5. Density declined, but surviving individuals generally increased in size.
6. Crematogaster presence was associated with damage, suggesting a biologically meaningful relationship that may merit further analysis.

## Reporting Notes

- Missing should not be treated as dead in any mortality estimate.
- Drought was grouped with natural drying damage where applicable.
- Some records in the tagged-tree file remain ambiguous because the status coding was mixed across explicit labels, missing heights, and comments.
