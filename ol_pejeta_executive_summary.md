# Ol Pejeta Conservancy Tree Monitoring Executive Summary

I reviewed the tagged-tree and density datasets for 2023 to 2025 using the methods described in the attached brief.

Two rules were applied throughout the analysis:

- Drought was treated as a damager category.
- Missing trees were excluded from mortality calculations because missing is not the same as dead.

## Key Findings

- Mortality in the tagged-tree dataset was 8.79 percent after excluding missing trees.
- Mean height change in trees alive in both years was 0.09 cm, which was not statistically significant.
- Excluded plots performed much better than non-excluded plots.
  - Excluded plots: mean growth 14.28 cm, mortality 1.01 percent.
  - Non-excluded plots: mean growth -0.77 cm, mortality 9.22 percent.
- Elephant and rhino were the main damagers, with damage height patterns matching expected browsing behavior.
  - Rhino damage was concentrated lower on the tree.
  - Giraffe damage occurred higher on the tree.
- Density declined significantly from 2023 to 2025.
  - 2023 mean density: 2,573.2 individuals per hectare.
  - 2025 mean density: 2,292.2 individuals per hectare.
- Height and length in the density data were strongly related, and both increased from 2023 to 2025.
- Crematogaster ant presence was associated with damage status in the density data.

## Rainfall Context

Annual rainfall was:

- 2023: 839.4 mm
- 2024: 1,120.8 mm
- 2025: 1,040.4 mm

This rainfall pattern helps contextualize the tree response, especially the lower drought-related pressure and the improved performance in excluded plots.

## Bottom Line

The main story is that tree survival and growth were substantially better in excluded plots, elephant and rhino were the dominant sources of damage, density declined over time, and missing trees should not be treated as dead in mortality estimates.
